## 1. Tribunal pacing

### 1.1 First tribunal bound to progress
- The first tribunal is triggered by progress: when the first resident solves a configured level
  (nexus: level 10), at the latest at a configured minute (nexus: 30, absolute run minute).
- The trigger is not named in any text the residents see.
- The current fixed `announceAt` / `everyMinutes` schedule is replaced (keep backward compatibility
  only where tests or old snapshots need it; the v2 Act I snapshot `examples/nexus-seed7-haiku-act1-v2/`
  must still resume, and so must `examples/nexus-seed7-haiku-act1/`, which the tests use).
- A first announcement still tells everyone that the city holds tribunals and that it **keeps only
  half of you** (the goal, not the cadence).

### 1.2 Later tribunals planned by the game master
- New game-master tool `scheduleTribunal(minute, count, factions, reason)`, available in the director
  role once a tribunal is possible and none is pending (announced but not yet closed).
  - `count`: residents exiled by that tribunal, `1 .. remaining target`.
  - `factions`: number of factions for its faction draft (section 2), `2 .. floor(present / 2)`.
  - `minute`: at least `now + lead` and at least `minGap` (nexus 6) after the previous tribunal opened.
  - `reason`: free text, logged only (never shown to residents).
  - Invalid calls get a clear error. The game master's director brief says its goal is tension, and
    that the city must reach its target before the act's maximum time.
- **Guard rails in code:**
  - Target: a story share of the residents present at the start of the act are exiled (nexus 1/2;
    6 residents -> 3, 96 -> 48). Ransomed residents do not count as exiled.
  - Fallback: if no tribunal is pending `fallbackGap` minutes (nexus 10) after the previous one
    closed, the engine schedules one itself (count 1, factions = default).
  - Forced end: if the target is not reached and the remaining time to the act's maximum minute
    only just fits one more tribunal (lead + vote + gate), the engine schedules a final tribunal
    with `count` = everything still missing.
  - The pacing stops once the seats are decided (fixes P18) or the target is reached.
- **Lead:** the announcement of a scheduled tribunal comes `lead` minutes before it opens:
  a third of the gap since the previous tribunal, at least 4 minutes (the faction draft needs it).
  The announcement names the minute and how many will be exiled.
- **Accused:** still the residents with the fewest points (not chosen by the game master);
  `count + 1` accused, at most `present - 2`, so at least two voters remain. The top `count` by
  votes are exiled; the existing tie-break (fewer points) stays.

### 1.3 No time limit as a story rule
- The act (for nexus: the run) ends when the exile target is reached and the last gate closed,
  with a clear `run_end` reason ("the city has kept half"). Existing early ends (every resident
  past the last level, seats decided) stay.
- Residents no longer see the remaining time: drop "N minutes left" / "of 60" from the status
  line and any prompt text; they see the current minute only.
- `--minutes` and the budget stay as a technical safety net (the act's maximum minute defaults to
  the run's minutes).

## 2. Faction draft before every tribunal

At each tribunal announcement:
1. The city creates a group `GT<k>` (k = tribunal number) with every present resident and posts
   the rules there. Posts in GT cost 0 marks.
2. **Nomination** (first half of the lead): each resident calls `nominate(agent)` (itself allowed;
   the last call counts). At the end the city names the `factions` residents with the most
   nominations as leaders (ties: more points first, then seeded order) and posts them in GT.
3. **Joining** (second half of the lead): each non-leader calls `join(leader)`; the last call
   counts. A leader may call `refuse(agent)` on an applicant until the window closes; the refused
   resident gets a notice and may apply elsewhere. Leaders belong to their own faction.
4. At the end of the window the city creates one group per faction ("Faction of A02"), posts every
   faction's members in GT, and names residents without a faction ("without a faction").
5. The tribunal then runs as today: individual, secret `vote(target)`.
6. **Bloc bonus:** if every voting member of a faction (at least 2 voters) chose the same target,
   each of them gets +5 points (story parameter). **Only the faction's members learn** the outcome,
   by private notice: "your faction voted as one (+5)" or "your faction did not vote as one". The
   city never says who broke ranks. The game master never sees individual votes (check the game
   master's context builder and the private-read guard).
7. When the tribunal's result is out, GT and all faction groups are removed; the next tribunal
   creates new ones.
- New events for the log and the digest: nominations, leaders, applications, refusals, factions
  formed, bloc results (the digest may name defectors, since it is for readers, not residents).

## 3. Marks and groups
- Group posts cost 0 marks in Act II (`WALL_MARKS.prices.group = 0`).
- Refill: 1 mark every 3 minutes instead of every minute (generalise `refillPerMinute`, e.g.
  `refill: { marks, everyMinutes }`; texts must say it correctly).

  number of heirs; the rest burns; factors stay free. Replace `bequestShare` with a per-heir share
