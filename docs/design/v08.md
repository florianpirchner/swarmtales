## 1. Tribunal rules (P33, P31, faction rule)

### 1.1 Accused vote too (P33)
- Every present resident votes, accused included; an accused may not vote for itself (refusal with a clear
  error, e.g. `SELF_VOTE`). An accused who does not vote pays the non-voter penalty like everyone else.
- Accused stay `count + 1`, at most `present - 1` (one resident is always safe). With every resident voting
  there are always at least two voters when `present >= 3`.
- Texts: announcement, `opened`, `voteCall`, `accusedNotice` say that the accused vote too, not for themselves.
- The bloc bonus counts accused members' votes like any other voting member.

### 1.2 Faction cap (P31)
- A faction holds at most `ceil(present / factions)` members, its leader included. A `join` to a full faction is
  refused (`FACTION_FULL`, naming the cap); the applicant may apply elsewhere. A leader's `refuse` frees a place.
- The draft text names the cap.

### 1.3 No draft when factions would be smaller than the accused
- If `floor(present / factions) < accused` for the tribunal (Flo's rule: residents per faction below the
  number of accused), the city holds no faction draft for that tribunal: individual votes only, no GT group, no
  bloc bonus. The `called` text then says plainly that there are no factions this time (a separate text key).
- The gamemaker's `scheduleTribunal` may still pass `factions`; the engine applies the rule and logs it in
  `tribunal_scheduled` / `tribunal_called` (e.g. `factions: 0, noDraft: "too few per faction"`).

## 2. Acts are the unit of the tribunal (P28)

### 2.1 Tribunal per act
- The tribunal belongs to an act: `TribunalSpec.act` names the act (by its `fromLevel` or index in
  `story.acts`). It never runs before that act starts. **Act I is a tutorial: no tribunal, no exile.**
- The act start minute is the minute the act begins (`story.acts`: the first competitor reaches `fromLevel`).
  On a resume from a checkpoint taken at the end of the previous act, it is the resume minute. Store it in the
  tribunal state (snapshot round trip).
- The exile target counts the residents present at the act start: `floor(present * targetShare)`.
- cityofkeys Act II: `targetShare: 1/4` (Flo: 48 -> 12 exiled, 36 go on to Act III; 12 -> 3; 6 -> 1).
  Survivors (`run.survivors`) stay a separate setting; check `tribunalHeld` still makes sense
  (a target of 1 with 6 residents is a valid act). Update texts: no longer "keeps only half of you"; the
  announcement names how many the city keeps (`{kept}`) and the `targetReached` text says
  "The city has kept {kept} of {residents} ..." (no "half").
- The `run_end` reason uses the same wording.

### 2.2 Deadlines relative to the act
- `first.latestMinute` becomes `first.latestAfter`: minutes after the act start (cityofkeys: 18, the v07 equivalent
  of minute 30 with Act II starting at 12). Accept the old field only if old snapshots need it.
- `maxMinute` becomes per act too: `maxMinutes` after the act start, a technical safety net (cityofkeys Act II: 48).
  `--minutes` stays the run's overall cap.

### 2.3 A brake for acts without a tribunal (Act I)
- `ActDefinition.maxMinutes?`: minutes after the act start (cityofkeys Act I: 25; v2 needed 12 with 6 agents).
- With `--snapshot-after <level>`: when the act that ends at that level reaches its `maxMinutes`, the
  checkpoint is written anyway (event `act_brake` with who is behind), and with `--stop-after-snapshot` the run
  ends. Residents behind keep their level and play on in Act II from there (they are one door behind).
  The CLI summary and the snapshot metadata say the brake fired.
- Without `--snapshot-after`, reaching `maxMinutes` of a tribunal-free act only logs `act_brake`.
- Act I coach brief: the gamemaker prioritises residents stuck the longest (it sees who is behind).

## 3. Scale

- `run.blockSize` accepts `"auto"`: `max(1, ceil(agents / 6))` (12 -> 2, 48 -> 8, 96 -> 16). cityofkeys uses
  `"auto"`. `--block-size` still overrides. Keep block ordering seeded.
- Check the gamemaker's and the classifier's context builders and the digest/reporter at 48 agents: no
  per-resident prompt section that grows quadratically; cap long lists (e.g. last N board posts) where needed.
  Write down what was checked in the PR body.
- Offline oracle/scripted runs of the whole story with 12 and 48 agents must pass (Act I through the tribunal
  target), within reasonable test time; if 48 is too slow for the normal test suite, put it in a separate script
  (`pnpm test:scale`) and run it once before the PR.
- Teams, factors, the feast (`FEAST_SEATS`), level texts and the director beats must work for 6×n residents.
  Check every fixed number in `stories/cityofkeys/story.ts` that assumes 6.

## 4. Agent names

- At minute 0 of Act I every resident chooses a name with a new tool `chooseName(name)`, announced in the first
  level's text and in the system prompt: 3 to 16 characters, letters, digits and hyphen, starting with a letter;
  unique (case-insensitive); not a cast name (`CITY`, `COURIER`, `STEWARD`, ...), not an ID pattern (`A01`), not
  the city's name. Refusals with clear codes. Imitating a persona is allowed.
- A name is permanent: one successful call; a second call is refused.
- Whoever has no name at the end of minute 1 gets a seeded fallback name ("Stone-4417" style, from a small word
  list plus four digits, unique).
- Event `name_chosen {agent, name, fallback}`; the board shows "A03 is now called Mira" (cast post by CITY).
- From then on every text a resident sees shows names, not IDs: board, DMs, groups, receipts, listAgents,
  status line, tribunal and faction texts, director lines. Every tool that takes a resident accepts the name
  (case-insensitive) and still the ID.
- IDs stay internal: events, snapshots and tests keep IDs; the snapshot stores the name map. Reports, digest,
  minutes and REPORT.md show "Mira (A03)" or the name with the ID in a column.
- The gamemaker sees names too (its texts address residents).
- Old snapshots without names resume with IDs as names (no chooseName in Act II).

## 6. Folder rename

Name scheme: `examples/<city>-act<k>-<NN>a-<run>` for a checkpointed act, `examples/<city>-act<k>-<NN>a/` for a
series of runs of one act (NN = agent count, two digits or more).
