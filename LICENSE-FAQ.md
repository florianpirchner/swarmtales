# License FAQ

This file explains `LICENSE`. If it differs from `LICENSE`, `LICENSE` applies. It is not legal advice.

**Is Swarm Tales open source?** No. It is source-available: you can read, run, change and share it for
free, within the limits of `LICENSE`.

**Can I use it in my company?** Yes, for your own internal business purposes.

**Can I run a service on top of it for others?** No, not without a commercial license. This covers
hosted or managed offerings for several customers, free or paid.

**I am an agency. Can I set it up for a client?** Yes, if the client alone uses it and it stays internal
to the client.

**Can I sell the stories and novels it produces?** Yes. Output you create is yours.

**Can I fork it?** Yes, and you can share the fork for free and non-commercially. You cannot sell it,
rebrand it or offer it as your own product.

**Can I sell a plugin?** Yes. The code of a plugin you write against `packages/contracts` and
`packages/sdk` is yours to license as you like. Those two packages themselves are under `LICENSE`, like
everything else in this repository.

**Is the licence viral?** No. It covers only the licensor's software. Games, stories, mechanics, plugins and
other work you build with Swarm Tales outside this repository are yours, under any licence you choose (point 6
of the Additional Terms).

**Can I write a GPL plugin?** Yes, outside this repository. Plugins talk to Swarm Tales through the separate
interface of `packages/contracts` and `packages/sdk`. Your plugin's licence does not change the licence of
Swarm Tales, and loading or combining it grants no rights beyond `LICENSE`. Meeting the plugin's own licence,
for example when you distribute a combination, is up to the plugin's author or distributor. Inside this
repository the rule stays: no GPL and no other copyleft licence.

**Can someone rewrite it in Rust or Python and sell that?** Not if the rewrite is a port or translation of
our code, prompts, stories or docs: that is a derivative work under `LICENSE`. A truly independent
implementation that uses none of our code or text is not covered by any license. The name stays protected.

**Will it ever become open source?** Not planned. Contact the owner if you need other terms.
