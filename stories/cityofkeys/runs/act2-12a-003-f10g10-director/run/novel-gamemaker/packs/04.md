# Pack 04: The Count and the Receipt

Point of view: the Gamemaker, minutes 31-42.

## The plan (outline)

- He reads the square after the receipt is shown and sees Hedda named by Keeper-Six, Mira and Holdfast; he decides the last count must carry a contested chair rather than a quiet hand.
- He posts the two names on the block, Archivist and Stentor, and opens the bloc draft so three chairs must fight over the voters' five-mark bonus.
- He presses Archivist toward the unread door-eight delivery and Ledger toward a debt he can prove, but both refuse to make a false figure or a bought promise.
- He offers Wren a bargain for her ballot and her last word, and Wren refuses to be seen in the open; Holdfast refuses a bounty on a sealed ballot; Mira refuses to turn Archivist's refusal into a charge.
- The chairs close with Archivist, Kessler and Hedda; Bellringer says the record will name Stentor by his refusal to speak his ballot, and the square fills with stated reasons.
- At the count's opening the Gamemaker has a scene but not a script: Archivist and Stentor stand on the block, and every voter has chosen whether to speak or hide.
- The hook is the sealed ballot and the gate that will not wait for a clean answer.

## The Gamemaker and his team

- The Gamemaker: the city's maker of games. He thinks aloud, reads the residents, sends notes, sets ultimatums, calls tribunals and posts to the board. His recorded thoughts are his inner voice.
- The Courier: carries his notes to the residents (they sign as COURIER) and brings back their refusals.
- The Guard: stops a note that would betray what a resident privately thinks; the city records each note it stopped.
- The Scribe: reads every message and thought of the residents and names what it shows (hesitation, a threat, a plea, a lie); the Gamemaker reads its reads.
