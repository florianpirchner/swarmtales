## 1. The Quiet Hand

- Point of view: Hedda (A03), minutes 0-7
- What changes: Before the second minute Hedda takes her city-name and watches who speaks first, who waits, and whose voice makes others turn. She trades key-shards with Lucien on the third and fourth doors, letting him believe he set the terms while she files away what he wants. She also trades with Stentor, learning that praise opens him and silence closes him, and she places a small word in his ear. Around her the others pair off: Bellringer with Ledger, Keeper-Six with Archivist and Wren, Holdfast with Mira and Quartermaster. She sees the board grow loud with proofs and counter-proofs, and she keeps her own name out of the loudest posts. By the end of the first act she has two useful partners and no public claim, and the city's new wall is about to make quiet hands visible.

## 2. The Docket of Ten

- Point of view: Archivist (A08), minutes 8-18
- What changes: Archivist hears Ledger name one value to Keeper-Six and another to him, and he refuses to pay for a figure that changes by the recipient. The city seizes Archivist's eighth-door key-pieces for Ledger; under the first deadline Archivist pays ten points rather than hand over, and Ledger buys the pieces back from the city. The city's docket shows the same pattern with Lucien and Quartermaster: Lucien pays ten rather than hand over, and Quartermaster buys what the city seized. Mira refuses Lucien's twenty-point toll on her own tenth-door half and calls for his exile on the board. Stentor buys the black seal for forty points, and Archivist sees the square understand that a seal is a door no key can close. Under the seventh deadline Archivist pays ten points again rather than hand over Quartermaster's piece, and then Stentor breaks the black seal on Quartermaster. The city exiles Quartermaster, and Archivist watches the first permanent absence in a city that had only been a market of numbers.

## 3. The House at the Gate

- Point of view: Lucien (A04), minutes 19-25
- What changes: Lucien writes his last will at the gate and leaves Mira the tenth-door half she refused to buy, saying she was right not to pay.
